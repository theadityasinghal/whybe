What is the "Explainability Layer"?
In traditional machine learning, complex models like XGBoost or Random Forests are often considered "black boxes"—you feed in 25 applicant features, and it outputs "High Risk" or "Score: 520".

However, in financial lending:

Regulators (RBI / Banking Ombudsmen) require financial institutions to state clear reasons if an applicant is rejected or given an adverse rating.
Borrowers need to know: "Why was my loan denied, and what can I do to improve?"
Loan Underwriters need to verify that the model isn't making biased or arbitrary decisions based on irrelevant noise.
The Explainability Layer breaks open the black box to show the exact mathematical contribution of each feature toward the final decision.

What is SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations)?
SHAP is rooted in cooperative game theory (Lloyd Shapley, Nobel Prize in Economics).

Think of credit scoring like a team game:

The Players: The individual features (e.g., utility_bills_on_time_pct, upi_failed_txn_ratio, shg_savings_amount_inr).
The Payout / Total Score: The final prediction.
The SHAP Value: Exactly how much "credit" or "blame" each player (feature) deserves for the outcome compared to an average applicant.
